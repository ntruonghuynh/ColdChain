> **SYNTHETIC DATA.** Every record in this dataset is generated by the project's own simulator
> (`data/generator/coldchain_sim`). No real shipment, product, sensor, organization or person is
> described. Policy values are teaching values, not regulatory or clinical limits. Results computed on
> this data describe the simulator, not real cold chains.

## 1. Folder layout and roles

|---|---|---|

### 2.5 Missing-value semantics (applies to every file)

| Situation | How it appears | How it must be treated |
|---|---|---|
| **Missing reading** (never delivered: logger fault, random dropout, battery depleted) | The row is **absent** from `readings` and `context`. Nothing is interpolated or forward-filled. | The interval between the neighbouring readings is a *gap*. Gaps longer than 15 min are **UNKNOWN**, never in range (BR-03, BR-04). |
| **Delayed reading** (ocean coverage gap, failed upload retried later) | The row **is present** with its original `event_at` and a later `received_at`. | Evaluate by `event_at`. Delay = `received_at − event_at`. Not a gap once received. |
| Empty field in a present row | Empty CSV cell, NULL in the database | *Not reported*. Never replaced by a default. |

## 4. Generation methodology

### 4.1 Physical model (1-minute steps)

Product (logger) temperature `T` follows a first-order thermal model, integrated with explicit Euler steps of 1 minute:

```
T[m+1] = T[m] + c·k_cool·(S[m] − T[m]) + (k_res + (1 − c)·k_amb + k_door[m])·(A[m] − T[m]) + ε,  ε ~ N(0, σ_p)
```

| Symbol | Meaning | Configured range |
|---|---|---|
| `S` | Setpoint, with a slow sinusoidal load wobble (amplitude 0–0.3 °C, period 3–8 h) | 3.5–5.0 °C |
| `c` | Cooling effectiveness: 1 working, 0 lost (reefer failure, defrost cycle) | |
| `k_cool` | Coupling to the setpoint while cooling works | 0.03–0.06 /min |
| `k_amb` | Coupling to ambient while cooling is lost | 0.0012–0.0025 /min |
| `k_res` | Residual ambient influence while cooling works (15 % of `k_amb`) | |
| `k_door` | Extra ambient coupling while the door is open | 0.004–0.015 /min |
| `A` | Ambient: profile mean (linear change for `VARIABLE`) + diurnal sine (peak 15:00 UTC) + AR(1) weather (φ = 0.998, innovation sd 0.05 °C) | see below |

Ambient profiles: `TEMPERATE` mean 10–20 °C, `HOT` 26–34 °C, `COLD` −10–2 °C, each with a diurnal amplitude of 2–8 °C. `VARIABLE` starts at −2–35 °C and moves by at least 12 °C over the journey (a route that crosses climates). Defrost cycles run every 6–10 h for 10–20 min in every journey.

### 4.2 Observation model (5-minute samples)

- Samples at `departure_at + k·5 min` up to and including `arrival_at`. The nominal 5-minute sampling interval is an approved simulation assumption (2026-11-14) and was approved for Milestone 3 on 2026-11-15 (D-01).
- `observed = clip(round(true + bias + drift·days_since_drift_start + noise + spike, 0.1 °C), −40, 60)`, with noise ~ N(0, 0.05–0.15 °C).
- Calibration bias ±0.2 °C on every logger; fault bias ±1.0–3.0 °C, drift ±0.5–2.5 °C/day (scenario `SENSOR_BIAS_DRIFT`); spikes +4–12 °C or −4–8 °C on one or two consecutive readings.
- Missing readings: random dropout (0.3 % per reading, first and last sample excluded), logger-fault gaps (scenario `MISSING_READINGS`), battery depletion (logger stops until arrival).

### 4.3 Transmission model

The gateway uploads buffered readings every 15 minutes (first upload 1–15 min after departure). Network latency is 2–90 s. A scheduled upload fails with probability 2 %, and its readings wait for the next successful upload (`RETRIED`). During an **ocean coverage outage** (4–14 h, scenario `OCEAN_COVERAGE_GAP`) no upload succeeds. The logger keeps sampling. 2–20 min after coverage returns, one upload delivers every buffered reading with its **original `event_at`** and the upload's `received_at` (`BUFFERED_COVERAGE_GAP`). A final upload 1–5 min after arrival flushes the remaining readings. Missing readings are never delivered. Delayed readings are always delivered. The two are modelled separately.

### 4.4 Scenarios

Each journey has exactly one primary scenario. Counts are allocated by largest remainder from the configured weights, and ambient profiles by quota inside each scenario. The list is shuffled with the dataset seed before journey numbers are assigned.

| Primary scenario | Weight | Canonical count | What is injected | Journeys with a true policy excursion (canonical) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `NORMAL` | 0.20 | 40 | Normal variation only (defrost, wobble, noise) | 0 |
| `GRADUAL_WARMING` | 0.125 | 25 | Setpoint creep 0.25–1.2 °C/h for 3–10 h; corrected with probability 0.6, otherwise persists to arrival | 13 |
| `HIGH_EXCURSION` | 0.125 | 25 | Reefer failure 90–360 min (if ambient ≥ 20 °C) or wrong high setpoint 10–15 °C for 60–240 min; a second event with probability 0.2 | 24 |
| `LOW_EXCURSION` | 0.10 | 20 | Wrong low setpoint −3–1 °C for 30–240 min; a second event with probability 0.15 | 20 |
| `SENSOR_SPIKE` | 0.10 | 20 | 1–4 isolated spikes | 0 |
| `SENSOR_BIAS_DRIFT` | 0.10 | 20 | Bias, drift, or both | 0 |
| `MISSING_READINGS` | 0.10 | 20 | 1–4 logger-fault gaps (short 1–2, unknown 3–4, review 5–36 missing readings); battery depletion with probability 0.25; a hidden temperature fault inside a long gap with probability 0.35 | 8 |
| `OCEAN_COVERAGE_GAP` | 0.10 | 20 | Sea route, 4–14 h outage, buffered upload; a temperature fault during the outage with probability 0.5 | 13 |
| `DOOR_OPENING` | 0.05 | 10 | 2–6 door openings (2–10 min, or 10–30 min with probability 0.2) | 0 |

Secondary modifiers, drawn from a separate random stream so they never shift the primary scenario: door openings (probability 0.15), a secondary spike (0.05), a sea route without an outage (0.15), no ambient probe (0.05).

Canonical totals: 200 journeys (`COLD` 40, `HOT` 59, `TEMPERATE` 71, `VARIABLE` 30), duration 26.25–70.83 h (mean 48.53 h), 116,669 expected samples, 114,969 readings delivered, 1,700 missing, 2,184 delayed by a retried upload, 2,244 delayed by an ocean outage (largest delay 844.03 min). 93 policy excursions (70 high, 23 low) in 78 journeys. 418 missing-data intervals (372 short, 7 UNKNOWN, 39 UNKNOWN with review required), 19 of which overlap a policy excursion.

## 5. How labels are generated

### 5.1 Three layers (A-12, accepted 2026-11-15)

| Layer | Produced by | Where it lives | Contents | Who may read it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ground truth** | The simulator only | `ground_truth/` files; DB schema `ground_truth` (`journey`, `event`) | True product temperature (1 min), true ambient, door state, injected causes, sensor-error components, observation status, and the *reference labels* `TEMP_ABOVE_MAX` / `TEMP_BELOW_MIN` and `EXCURSION_HIGH` / `EXCURSION_LOW` | Dataset loader; one allow-listed M4 label builder (supervised targets, D-12); evaluation. **Never** feature extraction, detection, inference, LLM, operational API or frontend code |
| **Observed sensor data** | The simulated logger and gateway | `observed/` files; DB tables `shipment`, `sensor_assignment`, `ingest_batch`, `reading`, `context_reading` | Logger values *with* bias, drift, noise and spikes; missing readings absent; delayed readings with original `event_at` and later `received_at`; context. **No label of any kind** | Everyone, including detection and ML features |
| **Operational labels** | The running system, from observed data only | Operational tables added in later milestones (`incident`, `detection`, `data_gap`) | M3: UNKNOWN gap intervals, `DATA_GAP` and `TEMPERATURE_EXCURSION` incidents from the policy baseline. M4: ML predictions and scores. Advisory; they never authorize a release (BR-15) | The application and users; compared with the reference labels in M9 |

**Operational labels exist since Milestone 3**, in the operational tables `data_gap`, `detection`, `data_quality_warning`, `incident` and `incident_revision`, produced only from observed data by the policy baseline (`python -m app.monitoring replay`). None existed in Milestone 2. The same policy rule (BR-02) produces both a *reference label* (applied to the true temperature, here) and an *operational detection* (applied to observed readings, M3). They are different by construction. The observed series has sensor error, 5-minute sampling, gaps and delays. How far the operational detections stray from the reference labels is the quantity M9 measures. Enforcement: `backend/tests/test_ground_truth_boundary.py` fails if any module in `backend/app`, `ml/` or `llm/` other than the dataset loader reads the ground-truth models, the `ground_truth` schema or the `ground_truth/` files.

**Training-time labels vs inference-time features (D-12, approved 2026-11-15).** A supervised model needs two inputs during training: *features* (X) and *targets* (y).

- **Features** are computed from observed data only, in exactly the same way in training and in inference. A model that saw a ground-truth column during training would need that column in production, where it does not exist.
- **Targets** come from the reference labels in `ground_truth/` and are used only to fit and to score models (training, validation, evaluation). They are read by one allow-listed label-builder module (M4).
- At inference time no target exists. The model receives observed features and returns an advisory prediction.

### 5.2 Rules

1. **Physical truth.** The 1-minute true temperature series is the reference. Temperature labels never look at observed readings. Gap and delay labels (rule 4, `TRANSMISSION_DELAY`) come from the simulator's own record of which readings were dropped or held back. They never come from the observed values.
2. **Ground-truth excursion intervals** (`TEMP_ABOVE_MAX`, `TEMP_BELOW_MIN`): every maximal run of minutes with true temperature strictly outside 2.0–8.0 °C. Exactly 8.0 °C or 2.0 °C is in range.
3. **Policy-derived labels** (`EXCURSION_HIGH`, `EXCURSION_LOW`): the policy duration rule of `POL-CHILL-01` v1.0 applied to those runs: ≥ 30 min above 8.0 °C, ≥ 15 min below 2.0 °C. Values are read from the policy file, never hard-coded. Shorter runs stay as near misses (`meets_policy_duration: false`).
4. **Gap labels** (`MISSING_DATA.policy_gap_status`): the gap between the delivered readings around a missing run, classified with BR-03/BR-04: `> 15 min` → UNKNOWN, `≥ 30 min` → UNKNOWN with quality review required, otherwise `SHORT_GAP`. Because samples are 5 minutes apart, 1–2 missing readings give a 10–15 min gap (not UNKNOWN), 3–4 give 20–25 min (UNKNOWN) and 5 or more give ≥ 30 min (review).
5. **Cause labels** (`INJECTED_SCENARIO`): the intervals the simulator injected. The `cause` of every policy excursion lists the injected disturbances that overlap it. A test checks that each one has at least one.
6. **Window classes for ML** (`NORMAL`, `EXCURSION_GRADUAL`, `SENSOR_SPIKE`, `SENSOR_BIAS`, `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`, architecture §6) are **not** produced in Milestone 2. Milestone 4 will derive them from these events and slots, with the window rules recorded in the feature spec.

These labels are the **reference for evaluation**. They are not what the Milestone 3 baseline detector computes. The detector applies the same policy to *observed* readings at 5-minute resolution, so it sees noise, bias, spikes and gaps. The difference between the two is exactly what M9 measures.

**Duration conventions differ by design (BR-05, approved 2026-11-15).** A reference label counts minutes of the 1-minute true series (`[start_at, end_at)`, so 30 true minutes qualify). An operational run lasts from its first to its last observed reading, so 7 readings are needed for 30 min above 8 °C and 4 for 15 min below 2 °C, and any missing reading breaks the run. The reference labels are unchanged. Details: `docs/design/m3-baseline-detector-contract.md` §2.4.

## 6. Database mapping (import)

| File | Table | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `manifest.json` | `dataset_release` | One row with the full manifest, seed, versions, policy hash and fingerprint |
| `observed/shipments.csv` | `shipment`, `sensor`, `sensor_assignment` | `data_origin = SIMULATED`, `source_dataset_id` set; status stays `CREATED` (workflow is M5) |
| `observed/uploads.csv.gz` | `ingest_batch` | `business_ref = upload_id`, `source = SIMULATOR`, `record_count = 2 × reading_count` (reading + context); since migration 0003 `ingest_mode = IMPORT`, `duplicate_count = 0` |

Primary keys of imported rows are UUIDv5 values derived from business keys, so the same dataset gets the same IDs in every database.

The Milestone 3 detector and the offline evaluation replay these batches causally along `ingest_batch.received_at` (the recorded receipt time); a reading is never used before its receipt (C-09). The detector's gap measurements are named `bracketing_span_s` (exact time between the bounding readings, compared with the policy) and `estimated_missing_samples` / `estimated_missing_duration_s` (information only); the reference label's `observed_gap_min` corresponds to the bracketing span.

## 7. Known limitations of the synthetic data

1. **It is synthetic.** The simulator's assumptions decide what is "true". Detection results measure agreement with this simulator, not real-world performance. The brief itself says 200 journeys is a teaching scale, not evidence of statistical sufficiency.
2. **Simplified physics.** One lumped thermal mass per shipment, a single logger, no pallet position effects, no product thermal lag separate from air temperature, and no humidity, solar load or reefer control loop beyond a proportional pull to the setpoint.
3. **Labels follow the injected mechanisms.** Every true excursion comes from an injected disturbance. Spontaneous excursions do not occur (by design: NORMAL journeys have none). Real cold chains have causes not modelled here.
4. **Clean separation of fault types.** Spikes, bias and drift are simple parametric shapes. Real sensor faults (sticking values, intermittent contact, clock drift) are not modelled. Logger clocks are perfect: `event_at` is exact and never later than `received_at`.
5. **Ground truth at 1-minute resolution, observations at 5 minutes.** Excursion boundaries seen through 5-minute samples can differ from the true boundaries by up to one sampling interval. This matters at the 30-minute and 15-minute thresholds.
6. **Ambient and battery** are smooth synthetic profiles; there is no weather data.
7. **Identifiers and sites are invented.** Dates fall between 2026-09-01 and 2026-10-28 (departures up to 2026-10-25, journeys up to 72 h); nothing should be read into the calendar.
8. **Gzip bytes** are reproducible with the same zlib build (the Docker image). Checksums of the *uncompressed* content (`content_sha256`, `dataset_fingerprint`) are the portable reproducibility reference.
9. **Floating-point platform risk.** Regeneration produced identical content (same `dataset_fingerprint`) on Linux arm64 (the project image) and on Linux x86_64 (`python:3.12-slim` under emulation), as recorded in `docs/milestones/m2/raw/09-cross-architecture-regeneration.txt`. Other platforms or Python builds were not tested. A different libm could, in principle, round a value differently at the last digit. Use `python -m coldchain_sim verify <dir> --regenerate` to check.
